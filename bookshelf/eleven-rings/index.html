The full reflection

One of the most important lessons I took from Eleven Rings is that becoming successful in your field, especially as a leader, doesn't necessarily mean focusing exclusively on that field.

Sometimes, the most valuable insights come from disciplines that appear to have absolutely nothing in common with what you do.

Take music and data science, for example. On the surface, music has little to do with AI or advertising technology. But if you look beyond the surface and start thinking more abstractly, you can discover connections in how different disciplines approach patterns, structure, interpretation, and problem-solving.

The important skill isn't simply knowing more about unrelated subjects. It's learning to recognize the underlying principles they share and figuring out how to bring those ideas back into your own work.

That changed how I think about developing expertise. Being well-rounded isn't necessarily a distraction from mastery. Sometimes, it's what allows you to see solutions that someone focused exclusively on one discipline might overlook.

The second lesson that stayed with me was about communication.

I've always valued the ability to explain complicated ideas simply, ideally well enough that a five-year-old could understand them. But I realized that simplicity alone isn't the same as effective communication.

True communication means connecting an idea to the other person's understanding, priorities, and interests.

In my work in data science and AdTech, I might be excited about an advanced analytical method that reveals more about the paths consumers take before converting.

From a technical perspective, that can be fascinating. But a client or executive isn't necessarily interested in the sophistication of the underlying model.

They want to understand what the analysis means for their business.

Should they change how they allocate advertising spend? Is a particular campaign underperforming? Are there opportunities to improve results? What should they actually do next?

An explanation can be technically accurate and still fail if the audience doesn't understand why it matters to them.

My biggest takeaway: Leadership requires both intellectual breadth and the ability to translate complexity into meaningful action.

It's not just about knowing your subject. It's about recognizing connections others might miss and communicating those connections in a way that helps people make better decisions.
